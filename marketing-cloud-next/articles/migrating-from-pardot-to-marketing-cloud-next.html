Migrating from Pardot to Marketing Cloud Next: A Strategic Roadmap

Ganesh Babu Dushyendiran Senior MarTech Architect | July 25, 2026 • 7 Min Read

For nearly a decade, Salesforce Account Engagement (Pardot) has been the cornerstone of B2B marketing automation within the Salesforce ecosystem. However, Salesforce’s strategic focus has shifted to the core platform, unifying automation capabilities under the **Marketing Cloud Growth** and **Marketing Cloud Advanced** packages.

This transition is more than a rename — it is a complete replatforming. Legacy Pardot operates on its own backend database, syncing with Salesforce CRM using a connector bridge. Marketing Cloud Growth runs **natively** on Salesforce CRM using Data Cloud schemas, Flow Builder, and unified Einstein AI.

Three Major Architectural Differences to Master

Before migrating metadata or assets, your operations team must understand these fundamental architecture shifts:

1. Prospects vs. Unified Contacts

In Pardot, your database consists of "Prospects" who must sync to Leads or Contacts in Salesforce. In Marketing Cloud Growth, there are no independent prospects. Segmentations run directly on **Unified Individual Profiles** inside Salesforce Data Cloud, drawing from your entire Salesforce Contact database and external systems.

2. Engagement Studio vs. Salesforce Flows

Pardot's visual campaign builder, Engagement Studio, is replaced by Salesforce **Flow Builder** (specifically Campaign Journeys). Flows give you access to all core CRM objects and automation tools, eliminating the limitations of Pardot's strict step actions.

3. Content Builder vs. CMS Workspace

Pardot files, emails, and layouts move to the Salesforce **Unified Experience Cloud CMS** and native Content Builder. Email templates are built directly within the Salesforce lightning builder interface.

The 5-Step Migration Checklist

  1. Audit Existing Data: Archive prospects who have not engaged in over 365 days. Clean custom fields in Pardot to ensure you only map active, business-critical attributes.
  2. Set Up Unified CMS: Configure your Salesforce CMS channel first. Upload brand assets, logo files, and marketing media before drafting new email templates.
  3. Rebuild Lead Lifecycle in Flow: Translate your legacy Pardot scoring rules and automation rules into native Salesforce Flow automations.
  4. Establish Data Cloud Ingestion: Connect your core CRM Lead, Contact, and Campaign Member objects into Salesforce Data Cloud to enable real-time segment generation.
  5. Warm Your Sending Domain: IP addresses and sending reputation do not transfer directly. Begin sub-domain warming protocols in MC Next with low-volume, high-engagement campaigns.
Strategic Note: Do not attempt a quick lift-and-shift migration. Take this transition as an opportunity to restructure your campaign hierarchies and optimize dirty custom fields that have accumulated in your CRM over the years.
